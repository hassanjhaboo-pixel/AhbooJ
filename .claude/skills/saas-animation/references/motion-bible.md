# Motion bible: the critique for every SaaS ad

Score each section 1 to 5 after every render round. Anything under 4 gets fixed before the next round.
Sources: Thomas and Johnston, *The Illusion of Life* (the 12 principles), product motion guides from GoCardless,
Customer.io and SAP Fiori, Hassan's handwritten rules, `style-rules.md` and `brand-motion/references/anti-vibecode.md`.

## 1. Idea and story
- One idea the whole ad serves, sayable in a sentence ("everything comes out of the logo and goes back into it").
- Hook in the first second. The brand is on screen at once, or something is already moving.
- Every beat shows a real product truth (a real feature, real UI, real work), in a sensible order: tool, action, result.
- The ending tells the viewer what to do (App Store, URL). Start and end can rhyme (bookend).
- Vary the rhythm. Beats are not all the same length: quick ones (0.8s) next to held ones (3s).

## 2. Transitions
- No cross dissolves between shots. A piece of the outgoing shot becomes the incoming one (shape, colour, line or position).
- Deconstruct, then rebuild: split, peel, unfold, curl, snap. The viewer can follow one element through the cut.
- Anticipation before a big change: a 4 to 8% squash or a short pull-back one beat before it goes.
- Overlapping action: the next element starts before the last one stops. Staggers stay inside about 0.5s per group.
- The eye stays put. Whatever carries the transition sits where the eye already is (dead centre by default).

## 3. Timing
- Fast in, slow out. Arrivals take 0.3 to 0.6s; holds are long enough to read.
- On-screen text stays up for at least 0.5s plus a third of a second per word.
- Big moments breathe: hold a hero frame for 0.6 to 1s before the next move.
- First drafts are too quick. If a move needs explaining, slow it down by about half.

## 4. Easing
- Ease out for things arriving, ease in for things leaving, ease in-out for travel. No linear motion except a constant spin.
- One easing family across the film, so it feels like one hand made it.
- Use curves that cannot overshoot for positions (monotone), so nothing wobbles backwards. No bounces.

## 5. Direction and camera
- One slow camera push through the whole film, never reversing.
- No move goes one way and then back the same way on the same subject (zoom in then out, scale up then down).
- Something is always moving, even if it's only the push.

## 6. Composition
- Subject dead centre; captions low and centred in one safe zone; effects only around the subject.
- One spacing scale. Card gaps, padding and tile gutters all come from it.
- Small text contrast at least 4.5:1. Nothing important sits on a busy background.

## 7. Secondary motion and texture
- Parts of one object move at slightly different times (follow-through), so motion feels physical, not robotic.
- Restraint: secondary motion supports the hero, never competes. One hero per beat.
- Motion blur on fast moves, so frames don't strobe.

## 8. Craft and truth
- Real UI rebuilt faithfully (names, colours, controls); personal details removed.
- Text: brand font, sentence case, short, true. No invented stats or claims.
- Spec ads end with "Spec ad. Not affiliated with <brand>."
- Anti-vibecode list: no purple-to-blue washes, gradient headlines, emoji, Inter display type, glass cards,
  eyebrow badges, three-icon rows, fade-in-on-scroll entrances, em dashes or buzzwords.

## How to run a round
1. Render stills at every transition (start, middle, end of each) and a key-frame sheet at 2 fps.
2. Score sections 1 to 8. Write the three worst problems down in plain words.
3. Fix those three first, re-render the affected stills, then score again.
4. Stop only when every section scores 4 or more, then render with motion blur.
